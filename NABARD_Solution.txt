1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================
India has ~14.4 million SHGs covering ~17.7 crore households (NABARD SHG-BLP,
FY2023-24), thousands of FPOs, and millions of individual rural entrepreneurs
in dairy, poultry, food processing, handicrafts and rural retail. Banks
disbursed over Rs. 2.09 lakh crore to SHGs in FY2023-24 alone — yet financial
health monitoring of these enterprises is still manual (paper registers,
periodic field visits, subjective grading). Early signs of stress are missed;
interventions arrive after default, not before.

Meanwhile the raw signals needed to PREDICT stress already exist:
  • Daily UPI/digital transaction patterns (rural UPI adoption grew ~47% YoY
    in FY2025; 4M+ kirana stores accept UPI),
  • Public mandi price data (Agmarknet/e-NAM: 3,000+ mandis, daily prices,
    open API on data.gov.in),
  • District-level rainfall/weather data and forecasts (IMD open APIs),
  • Digitized SHG books (NABARD's own e-Shakti covers 12 lakh+ SHGs),
  • Seasonal/sector patterns that are well understood but never systematized.

MIRA fuses these into a single, offline-first, multilingual platform:

  FOR THE MICRO-ENTERPRISE: a dead-simple ledger (income/expense/savings/loan
  entry with voice + regional language support) that gives back a 3–6 month
  cash flow forecast, a plain-language "health signal" (Green/Amber/Red), and
  concrete suggestions ("Feed prices are rising 12% — lock in feed purchase
  now", "Your festival-season sales start in 6 weeks — build Rs. 8,000 buffer").

  FOR THE FIELD OFFICER / BANKER: a portfolio dashboard ranking every
  enterprise by predicted risk, with per-enterprise cash-flow forecast
  charts, early-warning flags (missed savings, repayment gaps, transaction
  slowdown, sector shock exposure), explainable "why is this flagged" panels,
  and suggested interventions — turning reactive collections into proactive,
  targeted support.

  THE AI CORE: an ensemble forecasting engine (seasonal decomposition +
  gradient-boosted trees + statistical baselines) predicting monthly net cash
  flow 3–6 months ahead per enterprise; a composite risk score blending
  cash-flow health, repayment behaviour, transaction-signal trends, sector
  stress, climate exposure and market-price shocks; a rule+ML early warning
  layer; and SHAP-style explainability so every score is transparent and
  actionable — a hard requirement for adoption by banks and NABARD.

Built as a Progressive Web App that works fully offline (IndexedDB local
store, background sync), runs its lightweight models client-side when
offline, uses only mock/simulated but realistically-calibrated datasets, and
needs no sensitive personal data (no Aadhaar, no phone-number-linked UPI
records — only aggregated transaction-pattern proxies).

2. PROBLEM UNDERSTANDING & WHY IT MATTERS (RESEARCH CONTEXT)
================================================================================
2.1 The scale of the underserved segment
  • The SHG-Bank Linkage Programme (SHG-BLP), started by NABARD in 1992 with
    500 groups, is now the world's largest microfinance programme: 144.22
    lakh SHGs, 17.75 crore families, Rs. 2,09,286 crore disbursed in FY24
    (44% YoY growth).
  • 10,000+ FPOs are being promoted under the Central Sector Scheme with a
    Rs. 1,000 crore Credit Guarantee Fund managed by NABSanrakshan (a NABARD
    subsidiary). FPO studies (NAARM 2025 impact evaluation) consistently
    flag "lack of timely financing" and weak financial records as the top
    constraints.
  • ~80% of Indians lack access to formal credit largely due to absent
    credit histories (MicroSave / Grameen Credit Score project). Bihar alone
    is building a credit database for 10 million SHG members — proof that
    institutions want exactly the kind of data infrastructure this hackathon
    envisions.

2.2 Why current monitoring fails
  • SHG quality assessment today = NABARD's "Panchsutra" grading (regular
    meetings, regular savings, internal lending, timely repayment, proper
    books) scored manually on a 70/100 threshold — a snapshot done at loan
    time, not continuous monitoring.
  • Field officers manage hundreds of enterprises each; visits are monthly
    or quarterly at best. Stress builds between visits.
  • NPA rates on SHG lending (2.05% commercial banks, ~4.7% cooperative
    banks, FY24) look manageable in aggregate but mask localized crises
    (drought districts, sector shocks like avian flu or feed price spikes)
    that early warning could pre-empt.
  • NABARD's e-Shakti project (12 lakh+ SHGs digitized, auto-grading,
    auto-loan-application generation) proved digitization works and reduces
    NPAs — but it digitizes RECORDS; it does not PREDICT. MIRA is the
    natural predictive layer on top of an e-Shakti-like record base.

2.3 The regulatory tailwind (why timing is perfect)
  • RBI is explicitly pushing lenders from collateral-based to
    CASH-FLOW-BASED lending for MSMEs (30th Standing Advisory Committee,
    Oct 2025; Account Aggregator loans crossed Rs. 1.6 lakh crore in
    FY2024-25 across ~1.9 crore loan accounts).
  • New RBI co-lending framework (effective Jan 2026) enables bank+NBFC
    partnerships targeting rural/priority sectors — these lenders need
    exactly the risk-profiling infrastructure MIRA provides.
  • UPI-powered digital lending initiatives (Budget 2024-25) legitimize
    transaction-pattern-based underwriting.

2.4 The data ecosystem is ready
  • UPI: 86.7% of rural population aged 15–24 used UPI in Q1 2025; rural
    adoption up 47% YoY; 2,163 crore transactions in Dec 2025; 4M+ kirana
    stores accept UPI. Transaction count/frequency/counterparty-diversity
    patterns are a validated cash-flow proxy for micro-merchants.
  • Market prices: Agmarknet + e-NAM publish daily min/max/modal prices for
    200+ commodities across 3,000–4,367 mandis, with an open REST API on
    data.gov.in (CSV/JSON).
  • Weather: IMD offers open APIs for district-wise rainfall (daily +
    historical), 5-day district forecasts, nowcasts, and Agromet advisories.
  • These are all non-personal, public datasets — perfectly matching the
    constraint that the solution must not rely on sensitive personal
    information.

3. KEY RESEARCH FINDINGS THAT SHAPE THE SOLUTION
================================================================================
F1. ML beats classical baselines for small-sample cash-flow forecasting.
    Studies benchmarking 9 ML algorithms vs ARIMA on small firm datasets
    found most ML methods outperform, with k-NN and gradient boosting
    (XGBoost/LightGBM) working well even with ~33 observations — critical
    because a micro-enterprise may have only 12–36 months of history.
    Hybrid strategies (statistical + LSTM + XGBoost ensembles) are the
    state of the art for SME liquidity forecasting.
    → DESIGN CHOICE: ensemble of (a) seasonal-naive / exponential-smoothing
      baselines, (b) LightGBM with engineered features, (c) optional
      lightweight neural model server-side. Never a single black box.

F2. Early-warning literature converges on gradient boosting + anomaly
    detection + SHAP explainability; unsupervised methods (Isolation
    Forest, autoencoders) achieve ~85% recall for risk-breach prediction
    on real-time digital signals.
    → DESIGN CHOICE: two-layer EWS = interpretable rule triggers (domain
      rules field officers already trust, e.g. Panchsutra violations) +
      ML anomaly layer on transaction/cash-flow patterns, with SHAP-style
      reason codes on every alert.

F3. UPI transaction-pattern features (volume, frequency, counterparty
    diversity, day-of-week distribution, credit-to-debit ratio) are
    accepted cash-flow proxies in Indian fintech underwriting, and can be
    used as AGGREGATED, ANONYMIZED indices — no personal data needed.
    → DESIGN CHOICE: simulate a "Digital Activity Index" per enterprise
      (daily txn count, avg ticket, inflow/outflow ratio, unique-payer
      proxy) as a first-class model input.

F4. Sector economics are highly heterogeneous:
    • Dairy: daily revenue (unlike crop seasonality) but margins squeezed
      by fodder/feed price swings; lean vs flush season (summer milk yield
      drops 15–30%); disease shocks (FMD, lumpy skin).
    • Poultry: maize + soymeal ≈ 65–70% of cost; extreme feed price
      volatility 2023–25; heat-wave mortality; avian-flu demand shocks;
      short 6-week broiler cycles → cash flow is high-frequency.
    • Food processing: raw-material seasonality, FSSAI/working-capital
      cycles, festival demand spikes.
    • Handicrafts: long receivable cycles, order lumpiness, festival/
      export seasonality, exhibition-driven income spikes.
    • Rural retail (kirana): steady but monsoon-dependent footfall,
      harvest-season liquidity surges in farm belts, credit-sales (udhaar)
      stress.
    → DESIGN CHOICE: sector templates with distinct seasonality curves,
      cost structures, risk factors and recommendation libraries.

F5. Offline-first PWA is the proven pattern for rural India (2G/3G still
    dominant in many belts): service worker + IndexedDB as local source of
    truth + background sync queue. 95% faster repeat loads, 100% data
    persistence offline.
    → DESIGN CHOICE: PWA (not native app) — installable, works offline,
      one codebase, runs on the Rs. 6,000 Android phones field officers
      and SHG members actually own; simple on-device scoring fallback so
      risk signals work even with zero connectivity.

F6. NABARD's own e-Shakti taught the lessons: data entry burden must be
    minimal, grading must be automatic, and the system must generate
    banker-ready outputs (auto loan applications). Its gap: no forecasting,
    no market/climate integration, no proactive alerts.
    → DESIGN CHOICE: MIRA deliberately positions as "e-Shakti++" —
      the predictive intelligence layer NABARD's existing digital public
      infrastructure is missing.

4. SOLUTION OVERVIEW — WHAT CASHMITRA IS
================================================================================
One platform, two faces, three engines.

TWO FACES (interfaces):
  A) Udyami (Enterprise) App — for the SHG treasurer / FPO manager /
     individual entrepreneur:
       - 30-second daily entry: income, expense, savings deposit, loan
         repayment (big buttons, icons, voice input, local language).
       - "Health Card": Green/Amber/Red status with one-line reason.
       - Cash-flow forecast: "Next 3 months you will likely earn Rs. X,
         spend Rs. Y" as a simple bar chart.
       - Alerts & advice: push/SMS-style cards, each with ONE action.
  B) Adhikari (Field Officer / Banker) Dashboard:
       - Portfolio heat-map: all enterprises sorted by risk score, filter
         by village/sector/risk level.
       - Enterprise 360° profile: financials, forecast chart with
         confidence band, risk drivers (SHAP-style), alert timeline.
       - Risk panel: active early-warning signals across portfolio
         (e.g., "7 poultry units in Block X exposed to 18% feed price
         rise"), intervention tracker.
       - Reports: bankable cash-flow statement + risk profile per
         enterprise (the "credit passport" a bank can underwrite from).

THREE ENGINES (the AI core):
  1) FORECAST ENGINE — predicts monthly net cash flow, income and expenses
     3–6 months ahead per enterprise, with uncertainty bands.
  2) RISK ENGINE — composite 0–100 MIRA Risk Score updated daily,
     decomposed into 5 sub-scores: Cash-Flow Health, Repayment Discipline,
     Digital Activity Trend, Market/Sector Stress, Climate Exposure.
  3) EARLY-WARNING ENGINE — rule triggers + anomaly detection that fire
     specific, named signals (see §7.3) mapped to recommended actions.

DATA IN (all mock/simulated for the prototype, schema-compatible with real
sources for production):
  • Enterprise ledger (savings, loans, repayments, income, expenses)
  • Digital Activity Index (simulated UPI-pattern aggregates)
  • Mandi prices (simulated Agmarknet-schema commodity price series)
  • Weather (simulated IMD-schema district rainfall/temperature + forecast)
  • Sector calendars (festival demand, crop cycles, lean/flush seasons)

Key architectural decisions:
  • PWA over native: installable, offline-capable, single codebase,
    zero-friction demo for judges (just a URL).
  • Mock data generators are SCHEMA-FAITHFUL to the real public APIs
    (Agmarknet JSON fields, IMD district rainfall format) — so switching
    to live data is a config change, not a rebuild. This is a major
    credibility point for the "digital public good" story.
  • Models are retrained/rescored in a nightly batch (cheap), with
    on-demand rescoring when new ledger data syncs.
  • Everything an officer needs for a field visit is cached locally
    before they leave network coverage ("Download my beat" button).

    inflow_outflow_ratio, unique_payer_index, weekend_share}
  CommodityPrice {commodity, mandi, district, date, min, max, modal}   [Agmarknet schema]
  WeatherDay {district, date, rainfall_mm, rainfall_normal_mm, tmax, tmin,
    forecast_5d[]}                                                     [IMD schema]
  Alert {enterprise_id, date, signal_code, severity, message, action,
    status: open|acknowledged|resolved}
  Score {enterprise_id, date, risk_score, subscores{...}, forecast[6],
    forecast_low[6], forecast_high[6], top_drivers[]}

6.2 Mock dataset design (the demo must feel REAL)
  Generate 24–36 months of daily history for 50–80 enterprises across the
  5 sectors and 3–4 districts, engineered so that the demo tells stories:
  • Base income = sector seasonality curve × enterprise size × trend
      - Dairy: flush season Nov–Feb (+20%), lean Apr–Jun (−20%), daily sales.
      - Poultry: 6-week batch cycles → lumpy income; feed cost = f(maize,
        soymeal price series).
      - Handicrafts: Diwali/Christmas/wedding-season spikes, long gaps.
      - Rural retail: steady + harvest-season bumps + monsoon dips.
      - Food processing: raw material cost follows mandi prices; festival
        demand spikes.
  • Inject realistic shocks into SOME enterprises (these become the demo's
    "Red" cases): feed price spike, deficient-rainfall district, member
    illness (expense spike + income drop), repayment slippage pattern,
    gradual digital-activity decline (leading indicator!).
  • Include healthy enterprises (stable "Green" majority) and borderline
    "Amber" cases (single weak signal) — so the dashboard shows a credible
    distribution, not uniform doom.
  • Weather series: 2 normal districts, 1 drought-year district, 1 excess-
    rainfall/flood-event district.
  • Price series: maize/soymeal with 2024-style volatility; milk procurement
    price steps; vegetable price seasonality.
  Ground-truth labels for validation: an enterprise is "stressed at month t"
  if (net cash flow < 0 for 2 of next 3 months) OR (missed repayment in
  next 3 months) — lets us report honest precision/recall for the EWS.

6.3 Production data mapping (documented, not built, for hackathon)
  Ledger        ← e-Shakti / NRLM LokOS / bank statements via Account
                  Aggregator (consent-based)
  Digital proxy ← merchant UPI aggregates via NPCI/PSP partnerships
                  (aggregated, anonymized indices only)
  Prices        ← data.gov.in Agmarknet API (live, free, open)
  Weather       ← IMD public APIs (district rainfall, 5-day forecast,
                  Agromet advisories)
  Sector intel  ← NABARD district potential-linked plans, e-NAM demand data

7. AI/ML MODELLING APPROACH
================================================================================
7.1 CASH FLOW FORECASTING ENGINE (3–6 month horizon)
  Target: monthly net cash flow (and separately income & expense) for
  months t+1 … t+6, with P10/P50/P90 bands.

  Feature groups (per enterprise-month):
    • Lags & rolling stats: net CF lags 1–12, rolling mean/std 3/6/12m,
      trend slope, month-of-year, festival-proximity flags.
    • Behavioural: savings regularity (entropy of deposit gaps), repayment
      on-time rate, expense/income ratio, cash vs digital share.
    • Digital Activity Index: 30/90-day txn-count trend, inflow/outflow
      ratio drift, payer-diversity trend.
    • Market: sector-relevant commodity price level + 3-month momentum
      (e.g., poultry → maize+soymeal cost index; dairy → fodder index &
      milk procurement price).
    • Climate: district rainfall deviation from normal (current + monsoon
      cumulative), heat-stress days, 5-day forecast severity.
    • Sector calendar: lean/flush flag, batch-cycle phase, festival window.

  Model ensemble (pragmatic, small-data-appropriate — per F1):
    M0 Seasonal-naive & Holt-Winters — honest baselines, also the
       on-device fallback.
    M1 LightGBM regressors (one per horizon h=1..6, quantile objectives
       for bands) trained ACROSS enterprises (pooled panel) so young
       enterprises borrow strength from sector peers — solves cold start.
    M2 (stretch) Global N-BEATS/LSTM — only if time permits; not required
       for a winning demo.
    Final = weighted blend, weights from backtest (expanding-window CV).

  Evaluation: MAPE / WAPE per horizon vs M0 baseline; pinball loss for
  quantiles; report per-sector. Honest target: beat seasonal-naive by
  25–40% WAPE on simulated data — and SAY SO in the demo (judges trust
  teams who show baselines).

7.2 COMPOSITE RISK SCORING ENGINE
  MIRA Risk Score (0–100, higher = safer) = weighted blend of five
  sub-scores, each 0–100, each fully explainable:
    S1 Cash-Flow Health (30%): forecast net-CF trajectory, months-of-
       runway (liquid savings ÷ avg monthly deficit), volatility.
    S2 Repayment Discipline (25%): on-time ratio, days-past-due trend,
       Panchsutra-style savings regularity (deliberately mirrors NABARD
       grading so bankers recognize it).
    S3 Digital Activity Trend (15%): 90-day slope of transaction index —
       research shows activity decline LEADS income decline.
    S4 Market/Sector Stress (15%): sector cost-price squeeze index from
       mandi data (input price momentum minus output price momentum).
    S5 Climate Exposure (15%): district rainfall deficit/excess, heat
       events, forecast shocks × sector climate-sensitivity weight.
  Bands: 70–100 Green (healthy), 45–69 Amber (watch), 0–44 Red (act now).
  A supervised LightGBM classifier (predicting the §6.2 stress label at
  3-month horizon) is trained as well; its calibrated probability is shown
  as "Stress probability (3m): 68%" alongside the transparent score —
  transparent score for trust, ML probability for accuracy, SHAP values
  reconciling the two.

7.3 EARLY WARNING SIGNALS (named, specific, actionable)
  Rule layer (interpretable, runs offline on device too):
    EWS-01 SAVINGS_BREAK     : no savings deposit in 2 consecutive periods
    EWS-02 REPAY_SLIP        : repayment missed or partial ≥1 cycle
    EWS-03 CF_DETERIORATION  : forecast net CF negative for ≥2 of next 3 months
    EWS-04 RUNWAY_LOW        : savings buffer < 1 month of avg expenses
    EWS-05 TXN_SLOWDOWN      : Digital Activity Index ↓ >30% vs 90-day norm
    EWS-06 INPUT_PRICE_SHOCK : sector input cost index ↑ >15% in 60 days
    EWS-07 OUTPUT_PRICE_DROP : sector output price ↓ >12% in 60 days
    EWS-08 CLIMATE_ALERT     : rainfall deficit >40% of normal in growing
                               window / heatwave forecast / flood event
    EWS-09 EXPENSE_SPIKE     : expense anomaly (>2.5σ from personal norm)
    EWS-10 CONCENTRATION     : >70% income from single buyer/source (FPO/
                               handicrafts risk)
  ML layer: Isolation Forest over the feature vector flags "unusual
  pattern" cases rules can't name (per F2, ~85% recall achievable);
  surfaced as EWS-99 ANOMALY with top deviating features listed.
  Every alert carries: severity, plain-language cause, ONE recommended
  action, and who should act (enterprise vs officer).

7.4 EXPLAINABILITY & RECOMMENDATIONS
  • SHAP values on both forecaster and stress classifier → "Top 3 reasons"
    chips on every score/alert (e.g., "▲ Feed cost +18%", "▼ Savings
    regularity", "▲ Rainfall deficit 45%").
  • Recommendation mapper: (signal × sector) → curated action library
    written in simple language, e.g.:
      Poultry + EWS-06 → "Feed prices up 18%. Consider: buy 3 weeks feed
      now; talk to FPO for bulk purchase; reduce next batch size by 10%."
      Dairy + EWS-08 (heat) → "Heatwave forecast next week. Milk yield may
      drop 15%. Ensure shade + water; hold off new loan EMI commitments."
      Any + EWS-03 → officer action: "Schedule visit; consider EMI
      restructuring window before default."
  • This closes the loop the problem statement demands: prediction →
    warning → SIMPLE ACTIONABLE INSIGHT.

8. SECTOR-SPECIFIC RISK MODELS
================================================================================
Each sector gets: a seasonality template, a cost-structure map, tracked
commodities, climate sensitivity weights, and a tailored action library.

  DAIRY
   • Income: daily; flush (Nov–Feb) vs lean (Apr–Jun) ±20–30%.
   • Tracked prices: fodder/feed index, milk procurement price.
   • Risks: feed inflation, heat stress (yield ↓), disease outbreak,
     dairy-cooperative payment delays.
   • Climate weight: HIGH (heat, fodder availability follows rainfall).

  POULTRY
   • Income: 6–8 week batch cycles (broiler) / daily (layer eggs).
   • Tracked prices: maize, soymeal (≈65–70% of cost), egg/broiler NECC-
     style rates.
   • Risks: feed price spikes (2023–25 volatility), heatwave mortality,
     avian-flu demand collapse, cold-chain gaps.
   • Climate weight: HIGH (heat mortality).

  FOOD PROCESSING
   • Income: festival-skewed; raw material cost follows harvest cycles.
   • Tracked prices: sector raw materials (oilseeds, pulses, fruits/veg).
   • Risks: raw-material seasonality, working-capital cycles, spoilage,
     FSSAI compliance costs.
   • Climate weight: MEDIUM (raw material supply).

  HANDICRAFTS
   • Income: lumpy orders; Diwali/Christmas/wedding/exhibition spikes;
     long receivables.
   • Tracked signals: order pipeline (entered), festival calendar.
   • Risks: demand concentration (EWS-10), receivable delays, raw material
     (yarn, wood, metal) costs, e-commerce competition.
   • Climate weight: LOW-MEDIUM (monsoon disrupts production/logistics).

  RURAL RETAIL (KIRANA)
   • Income: steady daily + harvest-season surges + monsoon dips.
   • Tracked signals: local agri income proxy (mandi arrivals), UPI index.
   • Risks: udhaar (credit sales) stress, stock dead-capital, quick-
     commerce encroachment, festival inventory misjudgment.
   • Climate weight: MEDIUM (farm-income pass-through).

Sector templates drive: (a) forecast features, (b) S4/S5 sub-score inputs,
(c) which EWS thresholds apply, (d) recommendation text.

9. CLIMATE & MARKET RISK INTEGRATION
================================================================================
  • District-level rainfall deviation (actual vs IMD normal) computed
    weekly; monsoon cumulative deficit feeds S5 and EWS-08.
  • Heat events: consecutive days tmax > sector threshold (poultry 38°C,
    dairy 40°C) → yield/mortality risk flags.
  • 5-day forecast severity → PROACTIVE alerts (before the shock, which is
    the whole point of "early" warning).
  • Commodity price momentum: 30/60/90-day slopes on modal prices from
    Agmarknet-schema data; cost-price squeeze index per sector = input
    momentum − output momentum.
  • Local market disruption proxy: mandi arrival volumes anomaly + digital
    activity index of retail enterprises in same block (a village-economy
    pulse — unique feature, judges will love the "block heat-map").
  • Scenario view for officers: "What if rainfall is 30% deficient?" —
    forecast re-run with shocked climate features (simple, powerful demo).

10.1 UDYAMI (ENTERPRISE) APP — design principles: one screen one job,
     icons over text, numbers in Indian format, voice-first optional.
  Screens:
   1. Home / Health Card: big Green/Amber/Red circle + one-line reason +
      savings balance + next EMI date.
   2. Add Entry: 4 big buttons (Income / Expense / Savings / Loan) →
      amount keypad → category icons → done. Target: <30 seconds.
   3. My Forecast: 6-month bar chart (income vs expense), plain-language
      summary ("Tight months ahead: Aug, Sep").
   4. Alerts & Advice: card stack, each with one action button
      ("Mark done" / "Ask officer").
   5. My Loan: outstanding, EMI schedule, on-time streak (gamified badge —
      builds the repayment-history story for future credit).

10.2 ADHIKARI (FIELD OFFICER) DASHBOARD — desktop + mobile PWA.
  Screens:
   1. Portfolio Overview: KPI tiles (total enterprises, Red/Amber/Green
      counts, alerts today, avg risk score trend), risk distribution
      chart, sector × risk matrix, village/block heat-map.
   2. Enterprise List: sortable/filterable table (risk score, trend arrow,
      last entry date, active alerts, sector, village) + search.
   3. Enterprise 360°: profile, ledger timeline, forecast chart with
      confidence band, risk score gauge + 5 sub-score bars + SHAP reason
      chips, alert history, intervention log, "Generate Credit Report"
      button (PDF: cash-flow statement + risk profile → the bankable
      "credit passport").
   4. Risk Panel: all active alerts across portfolio, grouped by signal
      type; bulk actions ("7 poultry units affected by feed spike →
      schedule group advisory").
   5. Scenario/What-if: apply climate or price shock, watch portfolio
      re-rank.

11. OFFLINE-FIRST & LOW-NETWORK DESIGN
================================================================================
  • PWA with service worker: app shell cached (cache-first), API data
    network-first with IndexedDB fallback.
  • IndexedDB (via Dexie.js) = client source of truth for ledger entries;
    every write goes local first, queued for background sync.
  • Sync protocol: append-only ledger events with client UUIDs + timestamps
    → idempotent server merge; conflicts resolved last-write-wins with
    officer-visible audit trail.
  • On-device intelligence when offline: rule-layer EWS (§7.3) and the
    seasonal-naive forecast baseline run in JS locally, so the Health Card
    and basic alerts NEVER require network. Full ML scores refresh on sync.
  • "Download my beat": officer pre-caches full profiles of their
    enterprises before field travel.
  • Payload discipline: JSON deltas only, gzip, <50KB typical sync;
    UI assets <2MB total; works on 2G.
  • SMS fallback (documented for production): critical Red alerts via SMS.

12. MULTILINGUAL & ACCESSIBILITY DESIGN
================================================================================
  • i18n from day one (react-i18next): English + Hindi shipped in the
    prototype; string files structured for easy addition of Marathi,
    Telugu, Tamil, Bengali, Odia.
  • Icon-first UI, minimal text, numerals with Indian digit grouping.
  • Voice entry (Web Speech API, hi-IN) for amount/category — optional
    but a strong demo moment.
  • Low-literacy support: color+icon coding (Green/Amber/Red universally
    understood), audio playback of alerts (TTS) as stretch goal.

13. PRIVACY, ETHICS & DATA PROTECTION
================================================================================
  • No Aadhaar, no PAN, no phone-number-linked transaction records; UPI
    signals only as AGGREGATED per-enterprise daily indices (count/value
    index) — compliant with the problem-statement constraint and DPDP Act
    2023 spirit.
  • Consent-first framing: enterprise owns its ledger; officer sees scores
    & aggregates; "Generate Credit Report" is enterprise-triggered/
    consented.
  • Explainability everywhere (no black-box denials) — aligns with RBI
    digital-lending guidelines direction.
  • Bias guardrails: score never uses gender/caste/religion; sector and
    geography used only via economic variables; document this in the
    model card.
  • Model card + data dictionary shipped in repo (digital-public-good
    hygiene; strengthens NABARD's DPG value-creation goal).

14. TECHNOLOGY STACK (HACKATHON PROTOTYPE)
================================================================================
  Frontend : React 18 + Vite, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Recharts,
             react-i18next, Dexie (IndexedDB), Workbox (service worker,
             background sync), installable PWA manifest.
  Backend  : Python FastAPI, SQLite (file-based — zero-setup, perfect for
             demo; Postgres-ready via SQLAlchemy), Pydantic schemas.
  ML       : pandas, numpy, scikit-learn, LightGBM, SHAP, statsmodels
             (Holt-Winters); joblib model artifacts; nightly batch script
             + on-demand endpoint.
  Data     : Python mock-data generators (seeded, reproducible),
             schema-faithful to Agmarknet/IMD/e-Shakti structures.
  Reports  : server-side PDF (reportlab/weasyprint) for the Credit Passport.
  Auth     : simple JWT with role claim (enterprise/officer) — demo-grade.
  Deploy   : runs fully locally (uvicorn + static build) — no cloud
             dependency, honouring the offline/low-network constraint even
             in the demo; optionally deployable to any host.

16. ALIGNMENT WITH NABARD'S VALUE-CREATION GOALS
================================================================================
  1. Enhanced credit flow: the Credit Passport (forecast + explainable
     risk profile) is exactly the appraisal artifact banks need for
     cash-flow-based lending (RBI's stated direction); mirrors Panchsutra
     so bankers trust it on sight.
  2. Credit-led rural development: on-time streaks + verified ledger
     history let grant-supported enterprises graduate to institutional
     credit — the platform literally manufactures repayment-capacity
     evidence.
  3. Digital Public Good: open schemas mapped to existing public data
     infrastructure (Agmarknet, IMD, e-Shakti/LokOS), model card, no
     proprietary data lock-in — designed as common infrastructure, not a
     product silo.
  4. Better beneficiary outcomes: every prediction ends in a simple,
     sector-specific, actionable suggestion in the user's language —
     insight that reaches the enterprise, not just the institution.

17. WHY THIS SOLUTION WINS (DIFFERENTIATORS)
================================================================================
  1. PREDICTIVE, not just digital: e-Shakti/LokOS digitize records;
     MIRA forecasts and warns — the exact gap the problem names.
  2. PROACTIVE climate/market fusion: alerts fire BEFORE the shock hits
     cash flow (5-day IMD forecasts, price momentum), not after.
  3. EXPLAINABLE by construction: transparent sub-scores + SHAP reasons +
     Panchsutra-aligned metrics → institutional trust, regulatory fit.
  4. HONEST ML: baselines shown, backtests reported, small-data-
     appropriate models — credible to technical judges.
  5. TRULY offline-first: health signal and alerts work with zero network
     (on-device rules + baseline forecast) — most teams will only cache UI.
  6. TWO-SIDED value: enterprise gets advice, officer gets triage, bank
     gets a credit passport — one data entry serves three stakeholders.
  7. PRODUCTION-CREDIBLE: mock data is schema-faithful to real public
     APIs; the path from prototype to pilot is a config change plus
     partnerships, and the document maps it (§6.3, §18).
